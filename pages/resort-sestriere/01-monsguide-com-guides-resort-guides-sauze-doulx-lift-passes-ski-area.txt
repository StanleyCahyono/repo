LIFT PASSES
One pass, eight resorts, and one of Italy's biggest ski areas

Sauze d'Oulx sits on the Via Lattea — the Milky Way — and the standard lift pass here covers the whole of it: 400km of piste, 313 marked runs and 70 lifts across 8 linked resorts, all skiable from the top of the town without taking your skis off. A six-day adult pass is €305 and a single day is €58.50. For an area this size, that is the story.

What you get for it is a particular kind of skiing. Sauze d'Oulx's own mountain is tree-lined and red-heavy — run after run cut through the larches above the town — which makes it one of the better places in the Alps to be when the light goes flat and the high resorts turn into a whiteout. Your day starts at Sportinia, a sunny plateau above the treeline where the ski schools meet, and from there the mountain opens west and south across the rest of the area.

This page covers all of it: every price on the current tariff from one day to fifteen, where to actually buy a pass once you are here, the cheaper tickets worth knowing about, the rules that now apply on Italian pistes, and how the ski area fits together — including the two free days at Montgenèvre and Bardonecchia that come with a longer pass, and the honest way to use them.

THE PASSES
Which pass do you actually need?

Three tickets are sold from Sauze d'Oulx and almost everybody wants the same one. Here is the honest split before you look at the numbers.

Via Lattea — the one to buy
The standard pass, and the one priced in the table below. It covers the whole Italian side: Sauze d'Oulx, Sestriere, San Sicario, Cesana, Claviere, Oulx and Pragelato, all linked, all skiable from here. Book a package and this is almost certainly the pass your tour operator hands you.
The two free chip extensions
Six consecutive days or more and your pass carries one Montgenèvre day and one Bardonecchia day, both loaded straight onto the chip — no coupon, no ticket office, nothing to claim. Two extra resorts for nothing, and the reason a six-day pass is better value than a five-day by more than the price difference suggests.
Via Lattea International — rarely the right buy from here
Adds Montgenèvre for every day, at €69 a day or €347 for six. Genuinely useful if you are staying at the Claviere end and plan to live over the border. From Sauze d'Oulx it buys unlimited access to somewhere a long way away, which is why hardly anyone here takes it.
Montiluna — not a Sauze d'Oulx pass
Covers the Cesana and Claviere sector only. You would have to drive down the valley to use it, so it is for people staying in those villages rather than anyone based here. Mentioned because you will see it on the tariff and wonder.
Our steer: buy the standard Via Lattea pass and make it six days if your trip allows. The per-day rate falls as you add days, and six is where both free extensions appear. If you want a second day in France on top of that, there is a cheap way to do it — and it is not the International upgrade.

BUYING IT
Where to buy a lift pass in Sauze d'Oulx

Four places sell passes in the resort, and none of them needs to cost you your first morning.

At the lifts
There is a ticket office at each of the lifts out of the town — so wherever you are staying, there is one on your way to the snow. This is where most people buy, and where the first-morning queue forms.
In the centre of town
There is an office in Piazza Assietta, in the middle of Sauze d'Oulx. Handy the evening before — buy it after dinner and you can walk straight to the lift in the morning.
Jouvenceaux
There is also an office at Jouvenceaux, though it is not always open. Do not plan your morning around it — if it is shut, use the machine.
The automatic machines — the bit people miss
Every one of those locations has an automatic ticket machine, and they sell passes even when the office is closed. Early start, late arrival, office shut — it does not matter. You are never stuck without a pass here.
The best option is online, before you travel. It saves two euros a day, and you are issued a QR code rather than having to queue. Every location listed above has a collection point where you scan the code and pick the pass up — so you arrive, collect and ski, rather than standing in a queue on the one morning of the week you most want to be on the snow.
NOT A FULL-AREA PASS
Cheaper passes worth knowing about

Not everyone in the group needs 400km of skiing. Several narrower tickets are sold here and three of them are genuinely useful.

First-timers — Invito allo Sci
Around €41.50 a day covers the three Sauze d'Oulx lifts a beginner actually uses: Sportinia, Jouvenceaux–Sportinia and Clotes. If it is your first week on snow there is no sense paying for an area you cannot reach yet — start here and move up when you are linking turns.
Park riders — the snowpark day pass
Around €44.50 a day for the Sportinia, Rocce Nere and Jouvenceaux lifts, which are the three that serve the park. Cheaper than the full pass if lapping features is the whole plan — though read the honest assessment of the park further down before you commit a day to it.
Non-skiers — ascent and descent
Around €19 gets a walker up and back down on Clotes or Sportinia for lunch and the view. One thing to know: you cannot come back down on Jouvenceaux — it is up only — so plan your return on Sportinia.
Everyone — the insurance question
Sci Noproblem at €3.50 a day is the simplest way to satisfy Italy's compulsory liability requirement if your travel policy does not already do it. Read your policy first; if it covers third-party liability and piste rescue, you do not need this.

Complete beginner, or coming back after years off? A few mornings with a qualified instructor will get you off the nursery slope far faster than working it out alone, and here that matters more than usual — the run home from Sportinia is a red, so confidence on a red is what unlocks the resort.

BEFORE YOU RIDE THE FIRST LIFT
The rules on Italian pistes

Italy tightened its ski law in 2022 and tightened it again for the 2025/26 season. Two of these are legal requirements that catch British visitors out, and both carry fines.

Helmets are compulsory — for adults too
This changed recently and a lot of guides have not caught up. Until 2025 the helmet requirement applied only to under-18s. It now applies to everyone, at every age, on skis, snowboard, telemark, sledge and toboggan. Failing to wear one is an administrative fine, and a repeat can cost you your pass for up to three days.
Third-party liability cover is compulsory
You must be insured against injuring somebody else on the piste. Any insurer can provide it, your travel policy may already include it, or you can add Sci Noproblem to your pass at €3.50 a day. Skiing without it risks a fine and having your pass withdrawn on the spot.
Off-piste means carrying avalanche kit — by law
Where snow conditions carry any avalanche risk, anyone skiing off-piste, ski touring or walking on snow must carry an electronic transceiver, a shovel and a probe. This is not advice, it is the statute. Owning it and knowing how to use it are different things — see the off-piste section below.
Rescue is charged to you
Mountain rescue in Italy is billed to the person rescued, and off-piste rescue costs considerably more than a piste callout. Check your travel policy names piste rescue and repatriation explicitly rather than assuming a generic policy covers it.
Sources: the governing statute is Decreto Legislativo 28 febbraio 2021, n. 40 — helmets at Article 17, off-piste equipment at Article 26, compulsory liability cover at Article 30. The extension of the helmet requirement to all ages was made by Decreto-Legge 30 giugno 2025, n. 96, converted with amendments by Legge 8 agosto 2025, n. 119, which removed the under-18 wording from Article 17. Both links go to the official Gazzetta Ufficiale text. This is a summary for visitors, not legal advice.
ON THE MOUNTAIN
Skiing the Via Lattea from Sauze d'Oulx

Your day starts higher than the town. Understand that one thing and the whole place makes sense.

Sauze d'Oulx sits at 1,509m, but the skiing proper begins at Sportinia — a wide, sunny plateau above the trees where the ski schools meet, the beginner area and magic carpet sit, and there are bars to stand outside on a good day. You reach it from the Jouvenceaux lift and a short blue, or from Clotes and Lago Nero and down piste 11. Everybody's morning goes through it, so it is the natural place to arrange to meet people.

From there the mountain rises to Monte Fraiteve, which is the junction for everything: west towards San Sicario, Cesana and Claviere, or south over to Sestriere. The skiable extremes run from 2,800m at the top down to 1,400m at the Cesana end, so the lowest links sit well below the town and are the first to soften in a warm spell. Sauze's own side is the tree skiing — red after red through the larches. The full layout is on our Sauze d'Oulx piste map.

Why there are greens in an Italian breakdown. Italy grades runs blue, red and black — there is no green, and an Italian blue does the job a French green does, so a beginner is not short of easy terrain here. The greens counted above belong to Montgenèvre at the French end of the area, which grades on the French scale. One pass, one map, two national grading systems — worth knowing before you read a sign and expect it to mean what it would at home.

The split tells you the rest: overwhelmingly blue and red, across 8 linked resorts. This is a mountain built for confident intermediates who want to cover ground — you can ski a week here without repeating yourself, once you hold a red comfortably. Our Sauze d'Oulx resort guide breaks the terrain down by ability.

Wind is the local hazard, and it changes your day rather than ruining it. The high links across Fraiteve and out west are exposed and they shut first in a blow — which can strand you two resorts from your bed. The fallback is the thing Sauze d'Oulx is quietly good at: the tree-lined skiing below Sportinia keeps running with shelter and visibility when the tops have gone. Check the lift status before committing to a long day out, and keep an eye on the last connecting lift home. The lifts run from 5 December 2026 to 11 April 2027 — the detail of what opens when is on our Sauze d'Oulx opening dates page.

On the local knowledge behind this page: Angie, who lives here too, spent her Crystal Ski career finishing as Area Manager for the Val di Susa — Sauze d'Oulx, Sestriere, Bardonecchia and Claviere, and over the border Montgenèvre, Serre Chevalier, Briançon and Les Orres. She ran this area for a living. The route notes, the border-crossing warning further down and the wind advice above come from that, not from a brochure. It also shapes what we tell people to put on their feet: a mountain this red-heavy and this tree-lined rewards a ski with some edge grip, which is worth saying to the shop when you arrange your hire in Sauze d'Oulx.

FREESTYLE
The parks, honestly

Two parks and one boarder cross across the whole area on your pass — which reads better than it rides.

The Sauze d'Oulx park
Off Rocce Nere (piste 8) from the top of Sportinia (7). We will be straight with you: it is short, it is a handful of rails and jumps, and the lift layout makes it slow to lap — you spend more of the session getting back round than riding. Fine for an afternoon of messing about; not a reason to pick the resort.
Montgenèvre — the better park and the boarder cross
The French end has the bigger, better-shaped park, running alongside the green run Sagnes, and the only boarder cross on your pass. It is the better of the two by a distance — and it is the one you can only reach on your free chip day, or by paying the daily extension.
The park-only day pass
Riding the Sauze park and nothing else? Around €44.50 a day covers just the three lifts that serve it. Features open and get reshaped with the snow, so check what is actually built before you plan a day around it.
What this means for a freestyle trip
Freestyle is the weakest thing about an otherwise strong ski area, and it is the lowest score on our resort rating for exactly this reason. If park is the point of your holiday, this is not the place. If it is one thing among several, the Sauze park will keep you entertained for an afternoon and the coach can get you to the good one.
THE FRANCE DAY
Montgenèvre: the free day, and how to actually use it

A pass of six days or more puts a Montgenèvre day on your chip for nothing. It is a real benefit — but how you choose to get there decides whether it is a great day or a long one.

Skiing there is an expedition. It is lift after lift after gondola, west across San Sicario and Cesana and out through Claviere to the border, and even strong skiers who know the way should budget around two and a half hours in each direction. Nothing about it is technically hard — it is simply long, and it is at the mercy of the weather, because the high linking lifts are the first to shut in wind. Get caught on the wrong side of a closure and you are looking at a taxi fare and a very quiet evening.

The Col Boeuf trap, and it catches people every season. Lift 47, Col Boeuf, is where the French border scan happens. You are still standing in Claviere, still on Italian snow, and it looks like part of the same run — but scanning through it burns your one Montgenèvre day. If you have wandered out that way for a look rather than committing to France, turn around before the scanner. Plenty of people have spent their free day getting halfway and coming home.
Take the coach and start skiing at half nine
Our own Ski Away Day runs on Thursdays and takes about 35 minutes from the Tourist Information Office in Sauze d'Oulx to the Montgenèvre cable car. You ski a full day on the French side instead of the back half of one, and you are not watching the last connecting lift all afternoon.
Want a second day over there? It is €33.50, not €347
If you already hold a Via Lattea pass of six consecutive days or more, a further Montgenèvre day is sold as a daily extension for €33.50. That is the answer for almost everyone who wants France twice — not the International upgrade, which costs several times more for a benefit you will not use.
Or ski it, deliberately, on the right day
If the forecast is settled, the visibility is good and you want the mileage, ski it. It is a proper adventure and the terrain out west is worth seeing. Leave on the first lift, agree a turnaround time before you go, and check the lift status at Claviere before you commit to the last leg.
What is actually over there
Montgenèvre holds the better of the two snowparks on your pass and the only boarder cross, plus a proper French village at the top of the pass and a noticeably different feel to the Italian side. It is the reason the free day is worth using at all.

THE OTHER FREE DAY
Bardonecchia, and the free shuttle that makes it work

The second extension on your chip is the easier one to use, because you do not have to ski to it — the two ski areas now run a free bus between them.

Bardonecchia sits over the next ridge and is not lift-linked to the Via Lattea, so for years the free day was more trouble than it was worth. That changed when the two operators put on the Vialattea // Bardo Ski Shuttle: a free, direct coach between the areas, running Thursday to Sunday with several trips a day. It picks up in Sauze d'Oulx at the Jouvenceaux–Sportinia lift base car park and near the public bus stop on Viale Genevris, and drops at Campo Smith in Bardonecchia.

There is nothing to book. You are handed a return ticket on board on the way out, which you show and give back on the way home, and seats go on availability, so the first bus of the morning is the safe one on a busy week. You need a valid Via Lattea or Bardonecchia pass to ride it.

Worth doing? Yes, if the snow is thin at home or the light has gone flat — Bardonecchia skis higher, has its own separate areas, and a change of mountain halfway through a week does more for a trip than another lap of somewhere you already know. The shuttle's operating dates and timetable are confirmed each season, so check the current schedule on the Vialattea site before you plan a day around it.
BEYOND THE PISTES
Off-piste & heli-skiing

There is more here after a fall than the piste map suggests, and the trees are the reason. This is a pointer, not a route guide.

The obvious one is Sportinia down to Prariond, through dense larch forest — the classic Sauze d'Oulx line and the one that holds soft snow longest after a storm. The other local favourite is the bowl from the top of Rocce Nere, dropping down to the right of the Boulevard piste to the bottom of chairs 11 and 12: mellow, forgiving, and it tracks out fast, so it rewards being early. Fraiteve is a different proposition — it looks inviting and it is genuinely tricky, and local guides are wary of it. Further afield there are the Motta and Motta Rossa couloirs at Sestriere, and Pitre d'Aigle behind Sestriere towards Pragelato, which is about an hour of skinning for the reward.

Heli-skiing is available here and it is arranged locally through mountain guides, subject to weather and snow, booked in resort rather than on a lift pass. It is legal in Italy and banned in France, which is one genuine practical difference this side of the border.

Please read this before you leave the piste. As set out above, Italian law requires a transceiver, shovel and probe wherever there is avalanche risk — and carrying them is a different thing from knowing how to use them under pressure. Off-piste skiing carries a real risk of avalanche and of serious injury or death, including for experienced skiers with the right kit. Go with a qualified local guide, read the day's avalanche bulletin before you commit, and remember rescue is chargeable here. FAQS
Sauze d'Oulx lift passes — common questions
How much is a lift pass in Sauze d'Oulx?

For the 2026/27 season, a six-day adult Via Lattea pass is €305 online at high-season rates, with a single day at €58.50. The tariff runs from one day to fifteen and the per-day rate falls the longer you buy. Buying online saves two euros a day against the ticket office, and low-season weeks are cheaper again.

How much is a Via Lattea ski pass?

The same as a Sauze d'Oulx one. There is a single Via Lattea tariff across the whole area, so a six-day adult pass costs €305 whether you buy it in Sauze d'Oulx, Sestriere, San Sicario, Cesana, Claviere, Oulx or Pragelato — the pass is not priced per village. What changes between resorts is which lift you start from, not what you pay. The Milky Way and Via Lattea are the same area under two names.

Which lift pass covers Sauze d'Oulx?

The Via Lattea pass, which covers 400km across 8 linked resorts — Sauze d'Oulx, Sestriere, San Sicario, Cesana, Claviere, Oulx and Pragelato on the Italian side, plus Montgenèvre in France. It is the standard buy and the pass almost every tour operator issues with a package.

Where can I buy a lift pass in Sauze d'Oulx?

There is a ticket office at each lift out of the town, one in Piazza Assietta in the centre, and one at Jouvenceaux which is not always open. Every location also has an automatic machine that sells passes when the office is shut. Buying online is cheaper and gets you a QR code to collect from any of those points.

Does the Sauze d'Oulx lift pass include Montgenèvre?

Partly. A Via Lattea pass of six consecutive days or more carries one Montgenèvre day and one Bardonecchia day, both loaded onto the chip at no extra cost. If you want a second day in France, a daily Montgenèvre extension is €33.50 — far cheaper than upgrading to the International pass.

How long does it take to ski from Sauze d'Oulx to Montgenèvre?

About two and a half hours each way for a competent skier, across a long series of lifts and gondolas through San Sicario, Cesana and Claviere. It is not technically hard, just long, and the high linking lifts shut first in wind. A coach transfer takes around 35 minutes, which is why our Thursday Ski Away Day exists.

What is the Col Boeuf lift and why does it matter?

Col Boeuf is lift 47, and it is where the French border scan happens. You are still in Claviere and still on Italian snow, but scanning through it uses up your one free Montgenèvre day. If you are only having a look rather than committing to a day in France, turn back before the scanner.

How do you get to Bardonecchia on a Via Lattea pass?

Bardonecchia is not lift-linked, but the two ski areas run a free Ski Shuttle, Thursday to Sunday, with several trips a day. It picks up at the Jouvenceaux–Sportinia lift base and near the public bus stop on Viale Genevris. No booking, seats subject to availability, and you need a valid pass to ride it. Operating dates are confirmed each season.

Are helmets compulsory in Italy?

Yes — for all ages. Until 2025 the requirement applied only to under-18s, but Decreto-Legge 96/2025, converted by Legge 119/2025, removed the age limit from Article 17 of Decreto Legislativo 40/2021. From the 2025/26 season every skier, snowboarder, telemarker and sledger on an Italian piste must wear a helmet. Failing to do so is a fine, and a repeat can cost you your lift pass for up to three days.

Do I need insurance to ski in Sauze d'Oulx?

Third-party liability cover is a legal requirement on Italian pistes under Article 30 of Decreto Legislativo 40/2021 — you must be insured against injuring somebody else. Any insurer can provide it, or the resort's Sci Noproblem policy costs €3.50 a day and adds medical cover, kit and repatriation. Mountain rescue is charged to you in Italy, so check your travel policy covers it.

Is there a snowpark in Sauze d'Oulx?

Yes, off the Rocce Nere lift above Sportinia — but it is short and slow to lap. Across the whole area there are two parks and one boarder cross: the Sauze d'Oulx park, and a bigger, better one at Montgenèvre which also holds the boarder cross. A park-only day pass covering the three Sauze lifts is around €44.50.

Is there a beginner pass in Sauze d'Oulx?

Yes. The Invito allo Sci ticket is around €41.50 a day and covers Sportinia, Jouvenceaux–Sportinia and Clotes — the lifts a first-timer actually uses. Worth knowing: Italy grades runs blue, red and black with no green, so an Italian blue is the easiest grade there is and does the job a French green does.

When do the lifts open and close in Sauze d'Oulx?

The Via Lattea runs from 5 December 2026 to 11 April 2027. The town sits at 1,509m and the skiing runs up to 2,800m, so the higher ground holds on longer than the runs back to the village, and not every lift turns on the opening day. Our Sauze d'Oulx opening dates page covers what actually runs in early December.
