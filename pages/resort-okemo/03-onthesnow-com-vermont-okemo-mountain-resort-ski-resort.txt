Offering southern Vermont’s highest vertical drop of 2,200 feet and a trail network that spans 5 distinct alpine areas, Okemo Mountain Resort is a family-oriented and family-operated resort. This part is designed for skiers of all types and levels. Plus, with its multiple terrain parks and Superpipe, freestylers can also enjoy everything that this ski resort can offer.

Where Is Okemo Mountain Ski Resort Located?
The resort is located 12 miles from Londonberry, 25 miles from Rutland and 120 miles from Boston, Massachusetts. The closest airports are Bradley International Airport and Manchester-Boston Regional Airport, which are about two hours away. Boston Logan International Airport is about three hours away.

Transportation

Okemo Mountain Resort is accessible via several regional airports, with Rutland Southern Vermont Regional Airport just 30 minutes away and Burlington International Airport approximately 1.5 hours by car. For those flying into Boston Logan or Albany International, the drive takes about 2.5 to 3 hours. Shuttle services and private transfers are available from select airports, and rental cars are widely used for flexibility. The resort is located off Route 103, with clear signage and well-maintained roads leading into Ludlow.

Once in town, guests can take advantage of free shuttle buses that connect lodging areas with the base lodges. Parking is available at multiple base areas, including Jackson Gore and the Clock Tower, with designated lots for day visitors and overnight guests. Ride-sharing services operate in the region, and ski-in/ski-out access is available at select slopeside accommodations, making it easy to get on the mountain without delay.

Okemo’s terrain is spread across multiple mountain areas, including Jackson Gore, Solitude, and South Face, each offering a distinct skiing experience. The resort’s summit reaches 3,344 feet, and the vertical drop of 2,200 feet allows for long, flowing descents. The 3.4-mile Mountain Road is the longest run and a favorite for cruisers looking to stretch their legs. Snowmaking covers nearly all of the 43.5 miles of skiable terrain, ensuring consistent conditions even during dry spells.
Beginners will appreciate the gentle slopes off the Sachem and Easy Rider lifts, where wide trails and mellow grades make learning comfortable. The resort’s learning areas are clearly marked and supported by ski school programs, making it easy for newcomers to build confidence. Families often gravitate toward the Jackson Gore area, which offers a mix of green and blue trails with convenient access to base amenities.

Intermediate skiers have plenty to explore, especially on the South Face, where trails like Rimrock and Heaven’s Gate offer scenic views and steady pitches. Solitude is another great zone for intermediates, with well-groomed runs and fewer crowds. The resort’s grooming reputation means even steeper trails are approachable for those looking to advance their skills.

Advanced skiers will find challenge on trails like Quantum Leap and Eclipse, which feature steeper gradients and natural terrain features. These runs are often less crowded and offer a more dynamic experience. The resort’s terrain parks also cater to advanced freestyle riders, with jumps, rails, and a halfpipe maintained throughout the season.

Expert skiers can test their edge control on trails like Double Diamond and Big Bang, which offer tight turns and sustained steepness. While Okemo doesn’t have extreme chutes or open bowls, its expert terrain is well-designed and rewarding for those who seek precision and speed. Tree skiing is available in select areas, with glades like Outrage offering a more technical descent.

Freestyle skiers and snowboarders will enjoy Okemo’s five terrain parks, including the signature Hot Dog Hill and the more advanced Black Out. With 39 rails, a halfpipe, and progressive features, the parks are designed for all levels and are regularly refreshed to keep things exciting. The parks are spread across the mountain, allowing riders to incorporate freestyle into their day without straying far from the main trails.

Inside Scoop

If you’re chasing fresh snow, head straight to the South Face after a storm. Trails like Loose Spruce and Rolling Thunder tend to hold powder longer, especially in the morning before the sun hits. These runs are tucked away and often overlooked, making them ideal for early laps.

For a quieter experience, explore the Solitude area midweek. With fewer crowds and a peaceful vibe, trails like Sidewinder and Tomahawk offer smooth carving and scenic views. The Solitude lift rarely sees long lines, and the terrain is perfect for relaxed cruising.

Want to maximize your vertical? Start your day at the Jackson Gore Express and work your way across to the Quantum Four. This route lets you cover a wide range of terrain quickly and efficiently, with minimal lift wait times if you hit it early.

Families should check out the Snow Stars program, which offers age-specific lessons and guided mountain tours for kids. The learning area near the Clock Tower base is well-equipped and easy to access, making drop-offs and pickups stress-free.

For après-ski, the Sitting Bull Lounge is a favorite. Located near the Clock Tower base, it offers live music, hearty food, and a laid-back atmosphere. Try the Vermont cheddar burger and grab a seat by the fireplace—it’s a great way to wind down.

Looking for views? Take a break at the summit and snap a photo from the overlook near the Top 63 trail. On clear days, you’ll see across the Green Mountains, and the descent from there is one of the most scenic on the mountain.

Freestyle riders should hit the Black Out park early in the day. The features are freshly groomed and less crowded, making it easier to focus on progression. The park crew keeps the layout fresh, and the flow from feature to feature is excellent.

Tubing is best in the early evening at the Jackson Gore base. The four lanes are fast and fun, and the area is lit for nighttime rides. It’s a great way to keep the energy going after the lifts close.

Want to avoid the lunch rush? Eat early at the Roundhouse or pack snacks and stop at the Sugar House mid-mountain. It’s quieter, and the outdoor seating offers a great spot to refuel without losing momentum.

Finally, don’t miss the guided snowcat tours offered on select weekends. These tours take you to less-traveled parts of the mountain and offer a behind-the-scenes look at Okemo’s snowmaking and grooming operations. It’s a unique way to experience the mountain beyond the trails.
