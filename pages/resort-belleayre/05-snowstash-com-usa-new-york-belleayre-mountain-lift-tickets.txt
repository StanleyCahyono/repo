THE OVERVIEW
MF
Michael Fulton
Melbourne-based skier and snowboarder with 50+ resorts across 5 continents. Specialises in Australian resorts and international resort comparisons.

Belleayre Mountain's summit reaches 3,428 feet, dropping to a base at 2,024 feet across 50 runs spread over 175 acres in the Catskill Mountains near Highmount, New York. Terrain splits 58 per cent intermediate, 22 per cent beginner, and 10 per cent each for advanced and expert, with average annual snowfall of roughly 96 inches. Nine lifts, including three quad chairs and a gondola, serve the mountain alongside one triple chair and three surface lifts.

Belleayre holds a genuinely distinctive place among New York's ski areas as the state's only publicly owned and operated ski resort, a status that has shaped its identity since inception. The Catskill Park setting, protected as forever-wild forest land, required a specific constitutional amendment to permit the ski centre's development, reflecting the careful balance struck between conservation and recreation in this region.

A bill allowing New York State to create Belleayre was introduced in 1945 and passed in 1947, enabling a public referendum permitting roughly 20 miles of trails to be cut for an intensive-use ski centre within the protected Catskill region. Construction began in 1949 under the New York State Conservation Department, with a crew of 135 men breaking ground and building three main trails designed by Art Draper, a 10th Mountain Division veteran, ahead of the winter 1950 opening featuring the state's first rope tow and chairlift.

Belleayre was managed by the Department of Environmental Conservation for decades before transferring to the Olympic Regional Development Authority (ORDA) in November 2012, joining Gore Mountain and Whiteface Mountain under the same state authority. GETTING THERE
Getting to Belleayre Mountain, and where to stay

Belleayre Mountain sits near Highmount, New York, in the Catskill Mountains, with a base elevation of 2,024 feet and a summit at 3,428 feet over 175 acres of protected forever-wild forest land. This Catskill Park setting places the resort within a region long balanced between conservation and recreation.

The Catskills region surrounding Belleayre offers a range of small towns, inns and restaurants, with the resort serving as a genuine draw for the broader area's tourism economy. The New York City metropolitan area lies within a manageable drive, making Belleayre an accessible day-trip or weekend option for downstate visitors.

New York State Route 28 provides direct access to the resort, running through the heart of the Catskills region. Winter driving conditions on this route can include snow and ice, though the well-travelled state highway is regularly maintained given its role as a major regional corridor. There is no direct public transport service to the resort itself.

Specific nearest commercial airport details are not confirmed in available resort information, though the resort's Catskills location keeps it within a reasonable drive of the broader New York City metropolitan area. Belleayre's accessible, publicly operated model continues to serve as an entry point to skiing for many downstate New Yorkers.
