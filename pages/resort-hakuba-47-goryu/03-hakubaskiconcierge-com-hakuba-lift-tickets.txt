Essential Guide to Hakuba Lift Tickets: Prices and Tips for 2025/2026

Hakuba Valley, just 3 hours west of Tokyo, is a renowned ski destination.

A literal conucopia for skiers worldwide.

With 9 resorts and over 200 runs, if you haven’t been, you are missing out. There’s something here for everyone – no matter your skill level.

With relatively easy access from Tokyo, Hakuba’s resorts in the heart of the Northern Japanese Alps are a ski experience you won’t soon forget.

To help you plan your trip more effectively, we have compiled all the lift pass information you need to book your Hakuba Lift Tickets.

So come out, experience Japan’s powder snow, and make some new memories.

Read on to find out more about Hakuba’s lift tickets.

Hakuba Season Passes

The Hakuba Valley Season Pass is the ultimate pass. You have unlimited access to all 9 ski resorts with endless opportunities to explore some of the world’s most legendary powder snow. The season pass also comes with several perks:

Ride the Hakuba Valley Shuttle for free.
50% off 1-day lift tickets at all Vail Resorts.

*Note: The Hakuba Valley Pass does not include night skiing.

Who’s it For?

If you will ski for 30 or more days in Hakuba, then the Hakuba Valley Pass is for you.

Hakuba Lift Tickets

The Hakuba Valley Lift Ticket is a combined pass for the valley. They give access to all 9 ski resorts and the Hakuba Valley Shuttle Bus. Passes can be purchased online through the Hakuba Valley website, your accommodation/tour provider, or any lift ticket office.

Ride the Hakuba Valley Shuttle Bus for free.
Adult prices apply to anyone Ages 13+
Child Ages 6-12
Usable at multiple resorts on the same day
Not valid for night skiing or night shuttle bus
Who’s it For?

The Hakuba Valley lift ticket is easy to use and includes access to the Hakuba Shuttle. It is ideal for customers who visit Hakuba for 3 days or more. The multi-day flex passes give you the freedom to take a day off mid-trip to explore (7 out of 10 days, etc.). The other added benefit of a multi-day pass is that you don’t have to line up at the ticket office every morning, which can be a hassle if you’re trying to catch first-tracks.

Kashimayari (2025/26 Rates Not Yet Released)

Kashimayari Ski Resort, at the very South end of Hakuba Valley, has 2 ski bases, one in Hakuba and the other in Omachi. The snow resort is smaller than many others in the valley, but it is also usually less crowded and caters well to families. The lodge at the top features a large dining area on the second floor, with restaurants, shops, and rentals located on the main floor. Runs funnel into the top lodge from all directions, making it an excellent spot for families with older kids who want to venture out independently.

Hakuba 47/Goryu/Iimori

One of the few interconnected resorts, Hakuba 47 and Able Hakuba Goryu use the same lift pass. Iimori has some of the best beginner terrain for young skiers, and Goryu’s wide-open run at the base will help nervous skiers feel more confident. Intermediate and advanced skiers will appreciate the upper and mid-mountain, which have some nice groomed runs. For more advanced skiers seeking a challenge, Hakuba 47 offers exceptional lift-accessed tree skiing (a safety course and pass are required), and the adventure course connecting 47 to Iimori is a hidden gem on a powder day.

Happo One

Happo One ski resort is the largest in Hakuba, boasting some of the valley’s most advanced skiing and snowboarding. Mogul skiers will revel in some of the steep, long thigh-burning bump runs, while powderhounds can take advantage of Alpin, Skyline, and Omusubi. Although primarily an advanced mountain, Sakka offers some suitable beginner terrain, and the plaza at the base provides child minding and excellent restaurants.

Iwatake (2025/26 Rates Not Yet Released)

What Iwatake lacks in height, it makes up for in beauty and charm. Boasting the best 360-degree views in the valley and offering a plethora of non-skier activities, Iwatake will take your breath away.
Snowboarders will enjoy riding the natural side hits and banks found all over the ski resort. Tree skiers and powderhounds will want to head to the backside runs.

Tsugaike

Tsugaike Mountain Resort is the best beginner mountain in Japan, hands down. There is nowhere else with as many wide-open beginner runs. The resort is a paradise for beginner skiers.
Intermediate skiers will enjoy the wide-open runs, and advanced skiers will revel in the off-piste powder tree skiing.

Cortina/Norikura

The legendary Cortina is where powder hunters flock. This resort, located at the Northern end of the Hakuba Valley, is renowned for receiving the most snow. Cortina is also a big draw for backcountry skiers with its easy-to-access terrain from the top lift. Norikura and Cortina share a lift ticket, and for a good reason. If you ski the backside of Cortina, you will end in Norkura and then lift-hop your way back.

Epic Pass

Hakuba Valley became a member of the Epic Pass in 2019. Epic Pass holders receive 5 days of unlimited access to all of the resorts in Hakuba Valley and several other popular ski destinations worldwide The Epic Pass is an excellent option for anyone looking to experience some of the best skiing and snowboarding in Japan. The Epic Australia Pass provides access to Falls Creek, Perisher, and Hotham, as well as 5 days of unrestricted skiing in Hakuba Valley and Rusutsu Snow Resort.

To use your epic pass in Hakuba simply bring the pass to any ticket window to receive your 5-day Hakuba Valley Ticket.

Buying Lift Passes

Season passes can be purchased online from the Hakuba Valley website. Day passes are best purchased at a ticket window or through your travel/accommodation provider.

Hakuba Valley Shuttle Bus

Hakuba Valley shuttles buses give access to all of the valley’s ski resorts. A ride on the bus costs 800Yen, but Hakuba Valley lift pass holders can ride for free.

Final Thoughts

If you’re looking for some of the best skiing and snowboarding in Japan, Hakuba is a must-visit destination. With expansive terrain across multiple resorts, excellent powder conditions, and easy access from major cities like Tokyo, Hakuba offers something for everyone. Whether you’re an experienced skier or just starting, there’s something for you in Hakuba Valley. So what are you waiting for? Get out there and experience the best of Japan!

FAQ
Is Hakuba better than Niseko?

Both Hakuba and Niseko are well-known for their world-class skiing and snowboarding conditions. Still, they offer different terrain and experiences. Ultimately, the best way to decide which resort is right for you is to try them both and see which one you like best!

Is there night skiing in Hakuba?

Yes, Goryu offers night skiing.

Is Hakuba good for snowboarding?

Yes, Hakuba has some of the best snowboarding in all of Japan. Hakuba 47 has the best park in the valley, including a half pipe. Iwatake is also popular with snowboarders.
