Whistler Blackcomb targets Nov. 20 opening as powerful El Niño looms
The resort’s earliest scheduled opening in years comes with a new Blackcomb chairlift, revamped dining and later early-season operating hours
	Luke Faulks

Aug 18, 2026 4:00 PM
	
	
	
	
	
	
Vail is adapting to permanent daylight saving time in B.C. by opening valley and alpine lifts 30 minutes later until January. Matthew Sylvestre
Listen to this article
00:05:52

Whistler Blackcomb is targeting Friday, Nov. 20 to kick off its 2026-27 ski and snowboard season amid Vail Resorts' anticipation for an unusually powerful El Niño.

Vail Resorts announced the date Aug. 18, while cautioning opening plans remain dependent on weather and conditions. The company has Whistler opening alongside Park City, Heavenly and Northstar, after earlier starts at several Colorado resorts.

“Our teams are hard at work preparing for the season ahead, and we’re excited to introduce a range of enhancements that will elevate the guest experience both on and off the mountains,” said Whistler Blackcomb COO Belinda Trembath.

“From lift infrastructure investments and improved gear experiences to new events and elevated dining options, guests will see meaningful improvements throughout the resort."

The Nov. 20 target continues a gradual creep earlier on the calendar. Whistler Blackcomb was scheduled to open Nov. 21 last year, Nov. 22 in 2024, Nov. 23 in 2023 and Nov. 24 in 2022.

After a thin start last winter

Last year illustrated just how provisional November dates can be.

Blackcomb opened Nov. 21 with limited terrain: the Blackcomb Gondola provided access to Jersey Cream Express and Wishbone, with downloading mandatory at the end of the day. Whistler Mountain did not open until Nov. 26.

That slow start was part of a difficult winter across much of Vail Resorts' Western portfolio.

By early January, North American skier visits were down 20 per cent year over year, with Vail reporting a slower start at Whistler and near-historic-low conditions in Tahoe.

The problems were particularly acute farther south.

Through April 19, Vail's North American skier visits remained down 14.9 per cent, while lift revenue was down 5.6 per cent, ski-school revenue fell 12 per cent and dining revenue dropped 11.7 per cent.

Vail Resorts later described 2025-26 as one of the most challenging winters in the history of its Western U.S. operations.

Resort revenue fell seven per cent in its fiscal third quarter, while pass sales for next winter were running about 10 per cent lower in units as of late May. Whistler Blackcomb, however, was among the markets showing stronger pass-sales performance than the hardest-hit Colorado, Utah and Tahoe destinations.

A potentially historic El Niño

This winter's wild card could push conditions in a very different atmospheric direction across parts of Vail’s U.S. portfolio

The U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) announced on Aug. 13 that an El Niño system was strengthening, with a greater than 90-per-cent chance of becoming a very strong event this fall and winter. Forecasters put the chance of the October-to-December event reaching a strength unseen in records dating to 1950 at 69 per cent.

El Niño begins with unusually warm water across the central and eastern equatorial Pacific, but its importance to skiers comes from what that ocean heat can do to atmospheric circulation thousands of kilometres away.

By shifting and strengthening the Pacific jet stream—the high-altitude corridor along which storms travel—it can favour wetter winters across portions of the southern U.S. while tilting the Pacific Northwest toward warmer conditions.

Stronger El Niños increase the odds of those broad patterns emerging, but they cannot predict snowfall at an individual mountain.

In brief, the same circulation pattern capable of boosting storm potential in California and parts of the southern U.S. can favour milder winter conditions farther north, including in western Canada.

Vail Resorts is nonetheless leaning into the possibility of a big winter.

“While no one can predict exactly what Mother Nature will deliver, the possibility of blockbuster snowfall has us feeling optimistic about the winter ahead,” said Vail Resorts Mountain Division president Bill Rock. “And while we'd love to see Super El Niño live up to the hype, we're also taking an aggressive approach to snowmaking to ensure this ski season is one to remember.”

New chair, new food, new hours

On Blackcomb, the Showcase T-Bar is being replaced by a quad chair intended to improve access to Horstman Glacier and Blackcomb Glacier terrain.

Food-service changes include the return of breakfast at Dusty's, a renovated Pika's at the Roundhouse with a new coffee bar, and revamped patio experiences at Steeps and Christine's. The resort also boasts “higher-quality ingredients and refined presentation” for ski-day staples like burgers, pizza, fries, hot dogs, chicken fingers, and mac and cheese.

Opening day will also look different after the province adopted permanent daylight saving time earlier this year. After making its final spring-forward adjustment in March, the province will remain on UTC-7 year-round, leaving winter mornings darker than under the old system.

As a result, Whistler Blackcomb plans to operate most valley and alpine lifts from 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. from opening day through Jan. 15, rather than the traditional 8:30 a.m. start. Most Snow School programs will likewise shift 30 minutes later until mid-February, while traditional lift hours are expected to return around the B.C. Family Day and Presidents' Day period.
