PLAN YOUR DAY WITH VAIL’S SNOW CONDITIONS AND WEATHER FORECAST

Our snow report is your pre-ski checklist. Before you leave your lodging in Vail Village or pull into the Lionshead parking structure, you can see exactly what’s waiting on the mountain: overnight snowfall, current base depth at Mid-Vail, which lifts are spinning, and how many of our 195+ trails are open and groomed.

Check the daily snow report first thing in the morning to plan your ski day around conditions. If there’s fresh snow overnight, you’ll want to head for the Legendary Back Bowls via Gondola One and Chair 4 for the Sun Up and Sun Down Bowls, or Chair 6, then 10, then 14 for the China Bowl. With clear skies ahead, check the grooming report from overnight on the status of the front-side cruisers off Gondola One and Avanti Express (Chair 2) for mid-morning. You can also check out the groomers in the Chair 11 area (Northwoods), the Mid-Vail area near Chairs 3 and 4, or for the beginners in your group, the Chair 14 area.

Vail averages 354 inches of snowfall each season, among the highest in Colorado. From December through April check the updated snow report to find where you’ll have best chance of finding quality snow conditions on the mountain.

SEASONAL SNOW CONDITIONS AT VAIL MOUNTAIN

Vail’s ski season typically runs from late November through early April, with the most consistent snow conditions usually arriving between early January and mid-March. Conditions can vary throughout the season, but each part of winter offers a different mountain experience depending on snowfall patterns, terrain access, temperatures, and grooming conditions.

Season Phase	Typical Timing	Snow & Terrain Expectations
Early Season	November–December	Terrain expands gradually as snowfall and snowmaking build coverage across the mountain. Base depths often reach 30–50 inches by the holiday season, with increasing lift access and limited openings in parts of the Back Bowls depending on conditions.
Peak Season	January–February	Vail typically sees its most consistent storm cycles during peak winter, often delivering 6–18 inch snowfall events throughout the season. Full access to the Back Bowls and Blue Sky Basin becomes more common, while base depths often range between 60–90 inches.
Late Season	March–April	March is often one of Vail’s snowiest months, commonly bringing 50+ inches of snowfall alongside longer days and softer spring conditions. Coverage across the mountain generally remains strong through closing day, especially at higher elevations.

For the best day-to-day understanding of current conditions, check the current snow report, 7-day forecast, webcams, and grooming updates together.
